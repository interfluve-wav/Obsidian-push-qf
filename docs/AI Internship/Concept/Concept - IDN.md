# IDN — Integrated Delivery Network

---

## What an IDN Is

An **Integrated Delivery Network (IDN)** is a health system that owns and operates multiple hospitals, clinics, and physician practices across a geographic region, all under unified financial and administrative management. IDNs are the dominant organizational structure for large US health systems.

Examples of major IDNs:
- **Mayo Clinic Health System** (part of Mayo Clinic)
- **UPMC** (University of Pittsburgh Medical Center)
- **Kaiser Permanente** (integrated payer + provider)
- **CommonSpirit Health** (~150 hospitals)
- **HCA Healthcare** (~180 hospitals)
- **Providence St. Joseph** (~50 hospitals)

---

## Why IDNs Matter for AI Medical Scribe Sales

Enterprise sales of AI medical scribes happen at the **IDN level**, not at the individual practice level. When Abrige signs a contract with UPMC, it is selling to the entire UPMC health system — all affiliated hospitals, clinics, and physician practices.

This has several implications:

**1. Top-down sales motion**
Abridge's go-to-market is a "land and expand" model within IDNs:
- Start with one flagship hospital or academic medical center
- Prove value via publications (KUMC, JAMA Network Open)
- Expand to other hospitals and clinics within the same IDN

**2. System-wide EHR integration**
IDNs typically standardize on a single EHR platform (most commonly Epic). When Abrige gets Epic integration certified at one UPMC hospital, it can deploy to all UPMC Epic hospitals.

**3. Multi-site pricing leverage**
Large IDNs have significant negotiating leverage on price. A 10-hospital IDN adopting Abrige will demand volume discounts that a solo practice cannot.

**4. The "champion" dynamic**
Within an IDN, a physician champion at one hospital becomes the internal advocate for Abrige at other sites. This is why Abrige invests heavily in physician champions and key opinion leaders.

## IDNs vs. Independent Practices

| Characteristic | IDN | Independent Practice |
|---|---|---|
| Decision-maker | System-level (CMIO, CEO) | Individual physician |
| EHR | Standardized | Variable |
| Sales cycle | 6–18 months | 1–3 months |
| Pricing leverage | High | Low |
| Switching cost | Very high (system-wide) | Low-medium |
